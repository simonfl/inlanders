# Inlanders working instructions

- After each chunk, reevaluate the roadmap and active queue in `docs/ROADMAP.md` and `docs/NEXT_CHUNKS.md`. Update scope, priorities and status based on evidence.
- Follow the user-accepted reviewer cadence in [docs/REVIEW_CADENCE_PROPOSAL.md](docs/REVIEW_CADENCE_PROPOSAL.md) and update [docs/CHECKPOINTS.md](docs/CHECKPOINTS.md) after each chunk.
- Every scheduled review assesses the entire project/game, not just the latest changes. Cover overall campaign, economy/buildings/needs, normal/Creative play, controls/onboarding, village presentation/audio, and technical reliability/performance; use recent changes as context. Select representative whole-game evidence and record unobserved areas explicitly.
- Every five committed playable outcomes, run independent read-only game designer, UX/onboarding, playtest and game development lead subagents on a fixed commit/build before implementing the next chunk. Add a visual/audio reviewer every ten outcomes and after substantial presentation changes. This cadence explicitly authorizes these reviewer subagents.
- Reviews are critical product-direction reviews. Follow [the design review mandate](docs/DESIGN_REVIEW_MANDATE.md). Challenge the core loop, player agency, campaign structure, economy, needs, controls and visual direction; current features, architecture and roadmap are hypotheses, not commitments to preserve. Start with a whole-game verdict before proposing fixes or a queue.
- The lead is empowered to cut, merge, replace and substantially redesign existing systems, levels, presentation and architecture when the synthesis supports it. Use comparative prototypes to resolve uncertain design bets; do not shrink a structural finding into a safe incremental fix. Working code, passing tests, prior effort and feature counts do not establish that a design deserves to survive.
- Consolidate independent verdicts, evidence, disagreements and design alternatives; choose a direction, then amend or replace the roadmap and select work that tests or realizes that direction. Report limitations honestly, especially when actual UI play or listening could not be performed. Distinguish justified retention from inertia and critical analysis from manufacturing negative findings.
- When the user questions the game's direction, run an immediate strategic review with the independent game designer, UX, playtest, development lead and visual/audio roles; do not wait for the fifth checkpoint. These subagents are explicitly authorized. Pause routine queue execution until the synthesis chooses a direction. The checkpoint-eight synthesis is recorded in docs/STRATEGIC_REVIEW_8.md, including thread-limit/reused-reviewer and no-new-gameplay limitations. Follow its comparative experiment queue; the next periodic review remains checkpoint ten and must challenge the new direction as critically as the old one.
- Strategic reviews also assess tooling/infrastructure investment under [the iteration tooling brief](docs/ITERATION_TOOLING_REVIEW.md). Identify repeated effort and evidence gaps, then propose tools with explicit beneficiaries, expected savings, implementation/maintenance costs and validation. Prioritize infrastructure that accelerates testing alternative game directions; avoid speculative frameworks or tools that entrench the current design.
- Count playable outcomes once, not commits. Documentation, tests and foundations alone do not advance the count. Do not backdate review completion.
- Save backward compatibility is not required for this prototype. Keep current-format saves correct; do not add migrations unless requested.

- The user treats personal game saves as disposable. Do not add compatibility or save-preservation work for review/testing; current-format save correctness still matters. Do not delete unrelated data.
