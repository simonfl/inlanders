# F40 — a recognizable New France village

User priority: lean much harder into New France aesthetics. Make this a visible art-direction programme alongside the next gameplay work, not an indefinite polish backlog. Extend [F34's reference palette](REFERENCE_PALETTE_F34A.md) and [the thematic brief](LES_HABITANTS.md).

## The scene we want

A small rural St. Lawrence settlement around the existing provisional **1670–1680** reference window. Low, substantial houses stand beside kitchen gardens and practical yards; cultivated strips extend toward woodland; paths connect homes, food work and a modest river landing. Weathered timber, rough masonry, warm earth, pale plaster accents, linen and deep water give the scene its palette. People carry, mend, cultivate and rest in places whose use is visible.

Aim for a village worth looking at with the HUD hidden. Give buildings depth through massing, recessed openings, roof thickness, chimney shapes, foundations and material contrast. Keep people, entrances and work readable at the ordinary camera distance. A few coherent, well-composed structures should establish the identity before a catalogue-wide remodel.

These are art proposals, not approved historical reconstructions. Use the existing date/place anchor; exact construction, roof coverings, clothing cuts, crops, boat forms and implements need dated references. The Canadian Museum of History emphasizes architectural variation by region, period and means, and describes timber construction in early St. Lawrence settlements. [Architecture reference](https://www.historymuseum.ca/virtual-museum-of-new-france/daily-life/vernacular-architecture-in-new-france/). Keep reference images with their date/location and reuse rights recorded; broad museum overviews are starting points for asset research.

## First implementation slice

**Start with F40a + the relevant parts of F40b/c alongside F39a.** Build one inhabited farmstead ensemble: two house variants, a usable furnished yard, adjacent working cultivation, a woodlot edge and a river-facing approach. Reuse current people/rules and compare the existing scene at the same population and camera. The player should be able to make a domestic improvement and see people use it. This is a coherent art/gameplay slice, not eight prerequisite projects.

Then carry the chosen language into the oven/work structures and landing, followed by clothing/activity and audio/UI refinement. F40g lighting can be tested during the first slice. Exact asset counts, colors, crop species, roof forms, music instrumentation and later order remain TBD as we inspect results.

## Acceptance

- With title and HUD hidden, does the scene have a coherent researched New France identity rather than relying on its name?
- Are houses visibly substantial and distinct from production structures at normal play scale, in both camera orientations?
- Do worked land, yards and shoreline form the place, with clear entrances and actual resident activity?
- Is making and keeping a personally preferred arrangement visually rewarding, including keeping gardens beside homes?
- Inspect 960/1440 views, motion, occlusion and dense-scene clarity. Audition actual quiet/busy sound; stills and cue counts cannot approve it.

Substantial presentation delivery triggers an independent visual/audio review. Documentation, reference boards and unplayable asset foundations do not advance the playable checkpoint count. No new resource chain, needs, ownership, inheritance or seasons are implied by this art track.
