# A village worth staying in

November 13, 2026. Strategic assessment requested by the owner: “Can we think a bit bigger?” Fixed review baseline: commit `3b5ca97`, browser build `1B01F85B1539`, playable outcome 262. This selects a direction and next experiment; it delivers no new gameplay and does not advance the count.

## Verdict and lead decision

We have promising ingredients but not a sustained game. Helping residents, clear progress, recoverable food concern, automatic work and a compact phone interface have owner support. Our larger structure still usually resolves into placing sufficient capacity and waiting. Repeated changes to the opening trade have addressed real faults without answering what happens after the village works.

Choose **one authored settlement that develops through connected decisions in the same place**. The pleasure to pursue is making a practical home for these people, seeing our decisions change their daily lives, and wanting to improve the particular village that results. Reconsideration can contribute; repeatedly making a good plan wrong is not the goal.

The larger target is a roughly 20–30 minute first village story, including time to consider and watch, with a clear ending and optional continued tending. This is a design budget, not a countdown, minimum runtime or promise of fun. The existing river level demonstrates that stretching simulation time does not create this experience.

## The whole story

1. **Make a livelihood.** A small inhabited riverside holding has limited provisions. Choose a useful food investment and protect a workable timber supply. Understand the land through actual work and deliveries. Mild, recoverable food pressure gives the work purpose.
2. **Make room.** An announced group of neighbors needs homes. Develop convenient land at the expense of some working woodland, or keep that woodlot and expand along a longer route. What already works stays useful. Arrival changes the scene and available labor as well as consumption; it is not simply a larger target number.
3. **Make this place better.** With the new arrangement in use, decide whether scarce timber should shorten a busy route, strengthen its livelihood, or remain available for further plans. People visibly use the result. Finish with a readable account of the people now living and eating here, not a service-exam score.

These are connected situations, not three feature releases or locked technology tiers. Relevant options are available when their geography permits them. Good anticipation is allowed to work. If every consequential order can be queued unchanged at the start and the remainder is empty, this proposed structure has failed; do not conceal that with unlocks or forced disasters.

The setting should be legible in the whole composition: a river landing, modest inhabited timber homes, cultivated strips, a retained woodlot and real journeys between them. Descendants remain story and motivation only. No seasons, inheritance, conquest, mandatory religion/education needs or calendar survival system.

## The next slice: Room by the river

Test the missing middle before rebuilding the opening. Start with four housed residents, functioning food and an established grove; four expected neighbors supply a clear purpose. A side channel has a working but roundabout crossing. Both banks are accessible from the beginning.

The player can clear part of useful nearby woodland for homes/food or preserve it and develop the farther clearing. One optional **six-log shortcut bridge** can later shorten the routes created by that choice. It remains visible and buildable from the start; there is no artificial second-act unlock. Keeping the old route and investing elsewhere must remain viable. Existing construction, walking, clearing and food rules provide the consequences.

This is a bounded 10–15 minute comparison target, not the complete story. Use two authored starting arrangements of the same situation to check whether site use actually changes the shortcut's value. Keep food supply and population comparable. Do not add storage, home furnishing, service needs, production chains or another merchant system to make the second decision appear deeper.

The ordinary goal is to settle and feed the expected people. An optional improvement must not secretly become a completion requirement. Exact reserves and arrival pacing will be set from the first mechanical comparison and displayed honestly. No new sustain timer. Existing efficient plans are not punished by a scripted breakdown.

Before polishing, prove that near/far approaches are viable, that land and travel differences matter, and that immediate shortcut construction is not always the obvious best purchase. If this fails, revise the map once or reject the slice; do not expand the rules to rescue it.

All roles warn against forcing reconsideration. The player's earlier work must retain value. A missed opportunity, a different site or a slower route should be recoverable; do not manufacture demolition or an unexpected shortage merely to demand another click.

## Alternatives and conditions for changing direction

**Short restoration anthology:** a distinct inhabited village, one practical recovery, clear payoff, finish before stability becomes empty. This is a substantial fallback, not a lesser game. Choose it if the connected village's later work feels imposed or repeat play adds no interesting ownership.

**Village-making sandbox:** emphasize composing and watching a pleasing place, with little survival pressure. Existing Creative work is a resource. Do not select it as primary yet: the strongest owner feedback supports purpose and helping people under mild pressure, not open-ended decoration.

**Larger economic city-builder:** many needs, industries and upgrades. Reject for now. It would multiply interfaces and maintenance before showing why the current small decisions are worth making.

Support the chosen direction if the player can describe a sacrifice they chose, notices a consequence in their own arrangement and voluntarily changes or watches it. Reject it if the understood experience remains an obvious initial queue followed by fast-forward, or the later goal feels like an arbitrary demand. If the decisions are clear but the place is unrewarding to watch, test presentation of that same scene rather than adding another economic system.
