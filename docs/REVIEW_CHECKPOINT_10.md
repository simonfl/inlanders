## Whole-game verdict

**Partially convincing. Retain local production, physical distribution and shared work as the leading experiment; reject broader rollout of the current finite provisioning task as the campaign design.** The simulation now better matches the village the player sees. The central pleasure remains arranging a working place and seeing its daily life improve. The evidence does not establish that the present campaign sustains that pleasure for a competent player.

All five disciplinary passes identify the same missing middle: successful garden, bakery and early-arrival plans become comfortably productive after their initial construction. The reserve corrects the wild-food false victory, but does not cause another meaningful decision. Do not increase its target, add residents repeatedly, or create more food-feasibility arms to manufacture depth.

The game also has two competing player experiences. The main menu privileges the old ten-level campaign while the chosen rules and more inviting landscape are buried among development comparisons. Detailed panels compete with the village, and completed-task actions can remain prominent when food is the actual problem. A prettier scene or another tutorial paragraph would leave those structures intact.

## Chosen next direction

**Build one two-act settlement around a real competing use of land, with the interaction needed to understand that choice.** Keep the existing introduction and meadow provisioning as controls/onboarding material; do not turn them into a ten-level template.

The first act establishes a productive, inhabited village. The second asks the player to transform a desirable part of that successful village: preserve an established productive/woodland corridor and invest in a farther district, or reclaim convenient ground and replace what it currently provides elsewhere. Both choices must retain something valuable, sacrifice something visible and use ordinary construction, goods and journeys. The result should be a lasting shared place. Exact sites and project geometry are the next design work, not a hidden commitment to a named building.

Test an informed player who builds the obvious first-act solution well. The second act must still require a decision. Reject the prototype if the original layout handles both acts unchanged, one alternative is simply slower/more expensive, or the difference only exists in service-accounting numbers. A required building list, higher reserve or another timed certificate does not satisfy the brief.

This gameplay change and its player journey form one coherent slice. The front door must identify the chosen game, the commitment must visibly state count/time/consequence before activation, and the relevant food/work/space consequences must be inspectable in the world. Keep detailed timers and demolition accounting behind deliberate inspection. Do not hide necessary information just to make panels smaller, or guide every player to one prescribed producer.

## Keep, cut and resolve

- Keep physical construction/recovery, local grain/food, shared work, visible arrivals and the welcome. They have concrete causal and expressive value.
- Keep Creative's arranging tools. Their feature count does not establish appeal; no current Creative walkthrough was observed.
- Stop broad campaign rollout, new needs/producers, reserve tuning and generic ornament/music passes. Existing lake frontage, stone distance and woodland/habitat ideas remain material for fewer distinct settlements.
- Move historical campaign/rule comparisons out of the ordinary starting path while retaining explicit comparison access. Do not silently present incompatible supply/staffing rules as the same normal game.
- **Preserve the user's all-buildings-available instruction.** The lead offered a curated catalog or consistent food rules. Reject feature gating; make the chosen local-output rule work consistently for the existing edible producer types before teaching it as a universal rule. Curate what a situation teaches, not what the player may build.
- Recreation/comfort remain optional hypotheses. Do not add more need destinations until their visible everyday life or strategic role justifies the complexity.
- Do not punish early invitations merely to manufacture a dilemma. Early gardens currently succeed sooner without hunger; put the next tradeoff in a lasting spatial investment.

The serious alternative remains expressive village-making with light optional production ambitions. Keep it available if the competing-use logistics slice again requires bureaucracy to become interesting. Retaining the original assessment campaign is the cheapest implementation path, but the review rejects it as the preferred product direction.
