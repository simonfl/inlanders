# Independent game-design review — A home on the river

Fixed candidate `c88ae6e`, build `2FB99C49141A`, November14,2026. Reviewer supplied earlier design advice but did not implement the candidate. This is an independent critical assessment, not an endorsement of that advice. Source inspected at the fixed commit. Evidence: current source, builder comparisons, independent ordinary-command PLAYTEST report, and existing direction review. No owner-play claim. Other role reports were read after the principal pacing/gating judgment was sent to the lead; their observations are corroboration, not independently repeated evidence.

## Verdict

**Partially convincing; deliver this as one provisional full level after bounded pacing/usability corrections.** The complete premise is now coherent: establish food, welcome people into the same place, then use that village to help another holding. Export actually consumes supplies; cancellation and the local food floor make the commitment understandable and recoverable. A deliberate boat departure gives the work an ending. These are legitimate reasons to retain the connected level for the owner's next ordinary play opportunity.

This is not proof of a substantial management game. The principal decision remains allocating a few buildings and woodland intentions, after which autonomous production may dominate. The new recipient and ending provide purpose, but cannot by themselves turn a long unchanged production tail into rewarding play. The user's request for fewer, larger handoffs authorizes finishing the level; it does not require pretending that every balance choice works.

## Strongest case against

Builder comparisons leave235–954simulation seconds after the last investment. The farming/building route settles at352seconds then finishes1306seconds without changing its plan. Calling this a20-minute level would reward the same waiting problem we are trying to solve. Conversely the independent mixed garden/dock run finishes571.7seconds after four building placements, a cargo choice and a late extra managed tree. That can be a worthwhile small complete level, but it is not evidence of20–30human minutes or sustained late decisions.

The two fresh-meal chapter gates add ceremony rather than agency. In the independent run all eight were housed/fed immediately on arrival, yet the next choice waited87.5seconds for personal fresh-meal clocks. The UI can explain this accurately and it would still be an unhelpful gate. Do not fix it solely with a tooltip.

## Required bounded revisions

1. Establish the livelihood through actual local delivery and a first fresh meal; do not wait for every founder's appetite clock to unlock the next chapter. Keep individual meals as observed life or descriptive feedback.
2. Allow cargo choice when the announced eight have arrived and are housed/fed. Remove the second all-eight fresh-meal credential. Good early housing/preparation must remain useful.
3. Diagnose the slow farming route with actual packing counters and an ordinary additional food-site counterfactual. Do not preserve its20-minute duration as a design virtue. Do not globally nerf fishing based on unmatched scripted layouts. If another reasonably placed food source materially improves surplus, expose the actual bottleneck in cargo details and let that ordinary improvement work. If it does not, repair the relevant route/work allocation or tune the authored manifest; no new production tier.
4. Stage the departure at a readable real duration even after accelerated play. The outgoing contribution is the level's central visual payoff, not an incidental notification.

The exact shipment quantities are provisional authored demand, not historically meaningful numbers. There is no reason to defend64/24 merely because tests pass. Preserve asymmetric contributions only if each supports a sensible approach; prefer less empty tail over padding to a target duration.

# Independent UX/onboarding review — A home on the river

## Verdict

The permanent chapter strip and deliberate dispatch give the player a comprehensible beginning and ending. Keep this as one full-level handoff and resolve intermediate usability problems internally, following the November 14 process correction. ## Strongest case against the direction

The new arc can still be an initial construction queue followed by reading counters. The builder's `comparisons.json` records last orders at the settled milestone, followed by roughly 235–954 simulated seconds before completion across four approaches. This is scripted comparison evidence, not boredom measurement or independent player behavior, but it is a serious counterexample to claiming sustained later decisions. The strongest positive difference from prior sustain certificates is that the final output really leaves the village and has an explicit recipient/purpose.

The player is taught what counts more clearly than what is worth watching. Fresh-meal goals distinguish delivered food from initial provisions correctly, but appetite timing remains mostly invisible. A built food source, delivered crop and unmoving fresh-meal count can appear stuck until the player opens Details and reads that residents eat when hungry. Do not solve this with another permanent paragraph. A concise current cause near the incomplete row, or selected-person appetite/status feedback, would connect the rule to a person.

## Findings and internal revisions

1. **Keep the three-chapter orientation.** `renderStory` presents every chapter from the start and marks current/finished state. `ReadVillageStory` supplies concrete current conditions. Relevant food/housing/grove actions remain available rather than becoming technology unlocks. Cargo is offered only when it belongs to the story; that is comprehensible progression, provided its coming purpose remains announced.
2. **Make arrival timing available from the opening.** The welcome names four expected people but omits the eight-minute deadline. The chapter-one checklist does not display the countdown; only chapter two does. A slow livelihood recovery can therefore hide the timing of an already announced arrival. Include the timing in the brief or compact chapter-one line. This is a source-confirmed clarity gap, not observed human confusion.
3. **Keep cargo accounting and recovery.** Packed quantities are separated from village stock, options explain the economic emphasis, cancellation returns unfinished cargo, and the details explicitly state timber priority and the food floor. Send has a visible blocking explanation, not only a disabled control or hover tooltip. The 16-food requirement is a dispatch/packing rule, not a guarantee that the reserve stays at 16 after meals; avoid stronger language.
4. **Fix stale rotation text.** `startMode` resets the internal rotation to zero but does not reset the Rotate button's angle text. After rotating a prior plan, a new plan can show an old angle on a zero-degree preview. Reset the label with the state. The footprint is authoritative, but contradictory orientation feedback is avoidable, especially when placing shore docks.
5. **Keep reversible, world-directed placement.** Preview/confirm/cancel, woodland-clearing explanations, optional shortcut preview and same-ground garden entrance changes suit touch use. The six-pixel drag threshold and pointer cancellation handling exist. Their actual finger usability is unverified in this review. Picking intersects the ground rather than the building mesh; tall-object tapping deserves particular attention in actual phone play.
6. **Keep completion and continued tending distinct and clear.** Cargo departure has a 12-second simulation journey, then a completion summary with actual exported amounts. Keep tending closes the ending while completion has paused the world; the visible Resume control is needed to continue living activity. An ending should invite tending without implying the scene is already running. Reloaded completion remains accessible through Details, even though the modal only appears on transition.
7. **Protect world area.** The chapter strip is compact; detailed cargo commitments are modal, and placement hides irrelevant shipment controls. Those are good structural choices. In the fresh 402×681 opening, the world occupies approximately 324 pixels vertically (48% of the screen), with both banks, cottages, grove, old crossing and cargo boat readable together. Main buttons are visible without scrolling. The chapter labels are very small but their distinct sequence remains visible. This is an acceptable starting balance, substantially better than a status sheet overwhelming the place. The bottom cargo strip duplicates quantities already in the objective list, however, and this extra fixed height still needs visual checking in the packing state. Do not add permanent readiness paragraphs as the default remedy.
