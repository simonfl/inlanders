# Les Habitants — campaign situations and building roles

Working concepts under the [theme brief](LES_HABITANTS.md) and [active roadmap](ROADMAP.md). Farming, living from the land and improving the present settlement are the gameplay focus. Descendants are narrative motivation only: no inheritance, genealogy, aging or handoffs. Seasons remain removed.

Prefer a first normal-rule settlement that teaches through useful actions, with an optional finish and continued play. Five concepts below provide future variety; they are not a committed five-level sequence, calendar, or generation story. Start with one. Exact dates, names, objectives, durations, costs and population remain TBD.

## Five situations to explore

| Working title | Practical problem | Approaches and buildings | Visible satisfaction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Une place à nous — A place of our own** | Establish homes and a workable livelihood on limited clear land near the river. | Kitchen gardens/fields near homes versus a mixed cultivated/shore/woodland supply. Use current homes, fields/garden, dock, gathering and paths; introduce only what the chosen approach needs. | A small occupied settlement visibly grows/collects its food and has a comfortable approach to homes. No automatic demand to reach twelve residents. |
| **Le pain quotidien — Our daily bread** | Productive ground lies away from the homes and food service. Is a processing investment worth it? | Grain/bakery with sensible routes versus a simpler cultivated-food mix supported by the shore. Mill/barn candidates are optional extensions only after a useful siting decision is established. | Residents collect the resulting food; field work, processing and journeys make the investment understandable. No stockpile certificate. |
| **Entre bois et rivière — Between woods and river** | More building/farming space requires clearing useful woodland or using more awkward shore ground. | Preserve a woodlot and use existing fields/shore; clear selected ground, organize timber work and cultivate it. Existing preservation/habitat/paths give consequences. | A deliberately shaped balance of worked land, retained trees and useful access; recover from excessive clearing without restarting. |
| **D'une rive à l'autre — Between two banks** | A small tributary or side channel separates useful ground from daily work. | Bridge/route/material storage choices versus developing accessible land first. Do not depict a one-tile bridge as crossing the St. Lawrence itself. Real freight by boat is a later candidate, not required scope. | An occupied, supplied area connected to the original settlement through actual journeys. No mandatory local pantry if another route works. |
| **Une vie meilleure — A better life here** | An existing village works but has awkward walks, crowded work yards or poor shared space. | Revise homes/paths, choose an occupied-home improvement, improve storage/access or create a modest common place. Keep economic consequences without prescribing a hall or every service. | People visibly benefit from an improvement the player chose; finishing or staying is valid. This expresses the future-facing theme without simulating descendants. |

A chapter introduces one unfamiliar relationship or building group, not a list of things to own. Keep the complete catalogue available. Later maps should create decisions and recoveries beyond the opening placement burst, not extend duration with quotas, compulsory growth, recency assessments or waiting for a bigger counter. Story and situation can imply struggle without adding surprise death/disaster systems.

## Existing palette through the new theme

| Existing systems | Intended role / next question |
| --- | --- |
| Cottage / lodge | Small dwellings and a larger home option; research scale and construction before selecting public names/models. Keep housing simple, no household simulation. |
| Vegetable garden / farm / orchard | Cultivated ground, crops and establishment investment. Does land shape/clearing create a real tradeoff? Orchard remains optional. |
| Bakery / sawmill / carpenter | Food processing, construction materials and useful improvements. Distinguish mill versus sawmill clearly; a new grain mill is not a rename that silently changes recipes. |
| Forager / hunting lodge / fishing dock | Complementary land/water use. Research portrayal; do not make wild-food abundance the default farming bypass or require every producer. |
| Stockpile / pantry / bridge | Actual materials/food/access. Barn/granary or landing presentation may reuse these roles; prove any additional rule before adding a type. |
| Square / seating garden / hall | Modest shared places and visible breaks. Reconsider decorative scale. A chapel identity may be appropriate to a later authored setting without a religion meter. |
| Quarry | A material opportunity when masonry improves the selected place, not an obligatory civilization tier. |

## Candidate additions, not commitments

Start by shaping existing fields, harvest storage and production routes. A small barn/granary, working grain mill, common oven, pasture or cargo landing could later deepen those choices. Decide whether each is a visual variant, replacement or new mechanic before coding it. Avoid extra resource chains whose only effect is more hauling or waiting. Education, religion, full trade markets and seigneurial administration do not become required systems because they existed historically.

Historical representation uses dated/local evidence from [the reference brief](LES_HABITANTS.md). Indigenous peoples and relations belong to the researched setting; this is not an empty-land narrative or a commitment to a generic diplomacy mechanic.

## Acceptance and current status

No themed level or asset set is delivered by this document. Current founding/court/Free arrangement and earlier campaigns remain playable as documented. [The pre-theme systems plan](CAMPAIGN_SYSTEMS_PRE_HABITANTS.md) retains previous ideas and findings, not another active queue.

For each selected situation, compare at least two plausible ordinary approaches and an imperfect recoverable arrangement. Record initial resources, actual food/material movement, useful land and resident activity. Observe whether a player understands the choice and voluntarily improves or watches the result. A green simulation test or first visitor cannot establish fun. Keep current-format saves correct; no compatibility work. Follow the established whole-game review cadence.
